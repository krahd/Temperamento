# Temperamento and the Double Life of a Score

A musical score can organise music for listening and also specify computational actions. In Temperamento, these readings share an inscription while attending to different properties. A change of register or voicing can alter the music while preserving the programme; other small changes can alter what the machine does. This planned suite asks what a score can say musically through the properties that its executable reading preserves, transforms or ignores.

## A suite of three studies

1. **Different music, one computation.** Contrasting musical realisations preserve computational behaviour while developing register, voicing and supported noncomputational material as compositional resources.
2. **Related music, different consequences.** Closely related passages produce different computational actions. Their composition explores the relationship between the perceptual prominence of a change and its consequences for execution.
3. **A score unfolding twice.** An audiovisual work develops the temporal relationship between a sounding score and its executable reading. Musical form and presentation make the two readings available to follow together.

The suite will be accompanied by annotated scores and digital musical realisations. Its contribution lies in the new compositions and in the particular relationship between listening and execution that they develop.

## Existing work and new composition

Temperamento already includes five [executable showcase compositions](SHOWCASE.md): *Hello, World! Prelude*, *Conditional Canon*, *Countdown Étude*, *Hanoi Three-Disc Study* and *Twelve Transpositions*. These works include scores, programmes, recordings and inspection materials. *Twelve Transpositions* already presents variants preserving one programme, while *Conditional Canon* includes ordinary score audio and a separate execution sonification.

The new suite develops this foundation through iterative scoring, listening, musical revision and audiovisual composition. The relationship between the two readings becomes material for phrasing, contrast, continuity and expectation across the studies. The finished musical repertoire and its presentation are the objects of this production phase.

## Production and presentation

Tomas Laurenzo will compose and produce the suite through his own digital studio practice, using Temperamento's existing compiler and its documented two-stave notation. The executable reading comes from the authored score file. Digital musical realisations give the score its sounding form; the audiovisual composition develops the relationship between those readings.

A student technical and creative assistant will support score preparation and revision, checks of intended execution after musical edits, musical rendering, recording and editing, and preparation of the audiovisual edition and documentation.

Production is planned for January–May 2027, with an online presentation accessible to the CU Boulder campus community in April 2027 and a final edition by 31 May 2027. The edition will bring together the three studies, annotated scores and digital musical realisations.

## Artistic context

Daniel Temkin's [Velato](https://github.com/rottytooth/Velato) provides a direct musical precedent: compositions encoded as MIDI act as executable programmes, and its documentation includes a LilyPond score-to-MIDI example. In Temkin's [account of Velato and Light Pattern](https://www.icp.org/perspective/light-pattern-building-an-algorithmic-photography), the significance of an inscription for a person can differ from the properties used by a compiler. *Light Pattern* also presents different photographic realisations of the same programme and places images alongside their computational interpretation.

These works establish a lineage for executable artistic notation and for the distance between expressive and computational readings. *Temperamento and the Double Life of a Score* develops new musical repertoire within that lineage, asking how those differences can acquire a sustained compositional form.
